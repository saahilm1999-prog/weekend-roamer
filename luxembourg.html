Luxembourg in two unhurried days

A small capital with big views, compact neighbourhoods and free country-wide public transport. This is the case for staying a little longer than a day trip.

By Weekend RoamerUpdated September 2026

On this pageWhy goDay oneDay twoPractical notesSources

Luxembourg City rewards a slower pace. Its historic core sits above the Pétrusse and Alzette valleys, so a short walk can become a viewpoint, a quiet lower-town lane or a fortification path without much effort. You can cover the headline sights in a day, but two days leave room to enjoy the city rather than simply tick it off.

Why make Luxembourg a weekend?

It is a particularly easy place to navigate. Buses, trams and standard-class trains are free throughout Luxembourg, including for visitors. That means you can use the capital as a compact base and decide on the day whether to stay in the city or head into the wider country.

Plan around the landscape, not a list. Start in the Upper Town, walk down towards Grund, then let a valley path or a café stop decide the rest of the afternoon.

Day one: the Upper Town and the old fortifications

Morning

Place Guillaume II to the old town

Begin around Place Guillaume II and take your time around the compact historic centre. This is an easy place to orient yourself before moving to the city’s viewpoints and fortification sites.

Midday

The Corniche and the lower town views

Follow the viewpoints above the valley. Pause when the views open up rather than trying to map every single stop; the pleasure here is seeing how abruptly the city changes level.

Afternoon

Bock and the historic core

Choose one substantial historic visit, then leave time to wander. Opening times and access can change, so check the city tourism office before committing to a timed slot.

Evening

Pick one square and settle in

Keep the evening simple: a meal, a slow walk and a view. The city centre is compact enough that you do not need a packed after-dark plan.

Day two: Grund, a museum or a wider-country detour

On day two, use the city’s scale to your advantage. Start in the lower town of Grund or take a route through the valley. Then choose between a museum-focused afternoon, an architectural walk or a short rail trip beyond the capital.

Option A: stay in town

Explore a museum or gallery and use the city’s official sights map to pair it with one nearby historic or architectural site. This avoids spending time crossing the city simply to chase a checklist.

Option B: use the free transport

If you have two nights rather than a rushed overnight stay, a short trip beyond the capital can turn this from a city break into a Luxembourg introduction. Pick one region and check the latest rail timetable and local opening hours before leaving.

Practical notes

  • Getting around: standard public transport is free across the country. Check any first-class rail exception or special service before travel.
  • Airport to centre: Luxembourg’s official visitor information places the airport about 6 km from the city centre; bus and tram are convenient, ticket-free options.
  • Best pace: use comfortable shoes. The striking views come with hills, stairs and changing levels.
  • Before you go: verify attraction hours, temporary closures and event dates with the official city tourism office.
