Sarandë beyond the beach

Use Albania’s southern Ionian coast as a base for blue water, ancient sites and small adventures inland — then give the promenade time to do what it does best: slow everything down.

By Weekend RoamerUpdated September 2026

On this pageWhy goDay oneDay twoPractical notesSources

Sarandë is the kind of coastal base that works best when you look beyond the shoreline. The town faces Corfu across the Ionian Sea, while the wider area puts beaches, archaeological sites, springs and hilltop views within reach. A beach-first trip is perfectly valid; two or three days lets you add more texture.

Why use Sarandë as a base?

Albania’s official tourism site highlights the combination of sea, history and nature around the city. Butrint National Park, the Blue Eye spring, the Monastery of the 40 Saints and Lëkurës Castle are among the area’s better-known draws. That mix makes Sarandë more flexible than a single-resort stay.

A summer reality check: the coast is busiest in peak season. Keep your plan flexible, reserve essential transport where possible and consider morning starts for popular day trips.

Day one: settle into the coast

Morning

Walk the seafront and learn the shape of the town

Begin with the promenade, a coffee and an easy orientation walk. This helps you decide whether you want your second day to be history-led, beach-led or a blend of both.

Afternoon

Choose one water stop

Avoid trying to “do the whole coast” in one afternoon. Pick one accessible beach or a short boat excursion based on conditions, season and your own energy.

Evening

Head up for a view

Lëkurës Castle sits above Sarandë and is one of the nearby landmarks named by the national tourism office. Check access and current opening details locally.

Day two: pair history with nature

Butrint National Park

Butrint is the obvious history-first day trip. Give it enough time to explore rather than treating it as a quick photo stop. Check official opening information and transport options in advance, particularly outside high season.

The Blue Eye

The Blue Eye is a distinctive natural spring inland from the coast. If you pair it with Butrint, build in transport time and do not overload the day. Two meaningful stops are enough.

Keep a free afternoon

For a coastal trip, unplanned time is not wasted time. Use it for a swim, a slow lunch or a walk along the shore when the heat drops.

Practical notes

  • Think in bases: stay in Sarandë, then choose one substantial outing per day.
  • Transport: confirm current local bus, taxi, ferry and boat arrangements with your accommodation or an official local source before travel.
  • Comfort: take sun protection, water and suitable footwear if you plan to combine town, ruins and nature in one day.
  • Respect places: heritage and natural sites are not backdrops. Follow access guidance, stay on paths and take rubbish with you.
